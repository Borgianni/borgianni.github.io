Luca Borgianni, Ph.D.

Architecting the Infrastructure for Large-Scale AI Systems

I build the resilient, high-throughput networking and orchestration layers required to scale multimodal AI inference and distributed computation. I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of Pisa and an Affiliate Researcher at Stanford University (CCRMA). I received my Ph.D. cum laude focusing on resilient data-driven networking.

The next barrier in AI is not algorithmic; it is infrastructural. My work bridges high-performance networking, machine learning, and systems architecture to solve the hard bottlenecks of large-scale intelligence. I use demanding real-world testbeds—from zero-latency networked audio to automated biomedical detection (TAM project, Stanford Medicine) and real-time enterprise cybersecurity—to build systems that execute complex inferences when bandwidth, latency, and synchronization are critical constraints.

Research & Engineering Vision

Scaling real-time intelligence requires moving beyond static hardware topologies to dynamic, zero-latency architectures. I build the operating systems, the routing logic, and the network orchestration layers that allow thousands of models and agents to collaborate seamlessly across the edge-cloud continuum.

🌐 AI Cluster & Network Infrastructure

Overcoming network congestion bottlenecks. Applying Reinforcement Learning (PPO) and multi-agent systems to dynamically orchestrate data streams and computing power.

⚡ Low-Latency AI Serving

Designing system architectures capable of sustaining real-time AI serving and semantic inference under strict, sub-millisecond latency constraints.

🎙️ Multimodal Inference Systems

Building pipelines for heavy, multimodal workloads (speech, audio, biomedical signals) as the ultimate stress test for synchronized distributed networks.

🔒 Zero-Trust AI Security

Deploying real-time neural networks at the network edge to secure enterprise VoIP infrastructure against generative voice fraud and deepfakes.
